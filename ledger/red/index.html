the seals

one row per batch. the batch file is embedded in this page; its sha256 is computed here and looked for in the seal text; the seal text's sha256 must be the checked_sha256 of a send line that sits BEFORE every line of the batch; the stronger binding, checkable with the batch file and the chain alone, is that text k sits at seal seq + 1 + k (contiguous, in file order). a batch that fails its seal is VOID and counted: listed here with its reason, its plantings out of the recount, its lines left on the chain (RED_AMENDMENT 2026-11-08 rule 3). "sha fragment counted" is kill gate (c): the seal's digest is a designed exclusion of the reader (the sha scrub), as is the token sha256 (digits glued to letters), so its line should count 0 numerals; a positive would need the line's unbound_shapes to say which exclusion gave.

the misses: counted-by-design forms

forms the pinned reader reaches by its own rule; expected is the design's count (1, or 2 where the form is two tokens by construction). counted: total equals expected. missed: fewer. extra: more (the reader counted something the planter did not plant).

the counted-by-design and excluded-by-design classes were checked against the pinned extract_numbers outside the gate before the catalogue froze (BUILD_LOG_3c.md section 2; one smoke text of 22:46Z went through the copy and its chain was deleted before the started line): the null result is the gate agreeing with that probe, not a recall measurement over unseen forms. what can move here is the open-question totals, a form added at a later started line without a probe, and any later reader.

the exclusions: excluded-by-design forms

forms a named rule of the reader leaves out on purpose (year, date, version, sha fragment, markdown structure). confirmed: total is 0. not held: the reader counted it. the line is split by what reached the reader: a text with a digit went through helpers/numerals.py and its 0 is the rule holding; a digit-free text (number words) was handed to no reader at all by the gate's pre-filter _ANY_DIGIT, so its 0 is by construction and is printed apart (RED_AMENDMENT rule 2).

the open question: forms behind the lab's OFF flags, and the disclosed blind spots

the lab's later reader (certify.py v0.15, PR #242) names these as behind flags it ships OFF by a recorded decision (bare magnitude, form noise, sentence-final, accounting sign) or the v0.13 band discloses them as not counted (scientific notation, letter-glued numbers, non-ASCII digits, numerals in code); two forms sit under no rule at all. the pinned snapshot carries none of the V15 flags. the totals here are observed, not graded: a count that moves when the reader is repaired, and that repair is itself an amendment with a date. the "mark" column names the finding: a non-zero total under "no rule" is a read outside any rule; a non-zero total under a disclosure of "unread" is a count the disclosure did not predict.

the anchor

the protocol's anchor text for the red chain's last line of a day, gate anchor <date> head <64 hex> seq <n>, is posted to NO chat: it is the file anchors/<date>.txt, stamped with OpenTimestamps, committed to the exhibit branch. the text follows protocol v0 and names no ledger: the file's path under ledger/red/ and the head hex are the binding to this chain. the chain's own kind: anchor line for a day is written after 00:00 new york of the next day, before any new batch; a text written before the day closes is provisional.

the lines

seq · day (new york) · utc · kind · process · decision · numerals total · entry_id (the leading 16) · what accounts for the line (seal / planted text of a batch / unattributed). the planted text for a send line is in the batch file whose seal precedes it; the line itself carries hashes and counts only, as the protocol says. sent_sha256 differs from checked_sha256 on allow_marked lines because the gate appends its marker trailer to the sent text; the count is over the checked text and the seal check uses checked_sha256.

what this is not


    

  • not darkflobi's traffic: every text here was planted by a script from a sealed list; the destination is a synthetic id whose hmac is under a salt made for the copy; nothing was sent to anyone.
    • 

  • not a truth check: the reader counts numerals; the planted sentences say nothing true or false about the world, and the gate's other legs (receipts, the word list) are not what this chain measures.
    • 

  • not a measure of the live gate's refusals: the copy runs in shadow (required_present: false on every line) at a synthetic destination with no reader behind it; a "would refuse" here is a shadow verdict over a planted sentence.
    • 

  • not a recall measurement of the reader: the counted-by-design and excluded-by-design classes were checked against the pinned extract_numbers outside the gate before the catalogue froze (BUILD_LOG_3c.md section 2; one smoke text of 22:46Z went through the copy and its chain was deleted before the started line): the null result is the gate agreeing with that probe, not a recall measurement over unseen forms. what can move here is the open-question totals, a form added at a later started line without a probe, and any later reader. the null result under the misses table is the gate agreeing with that probe.
    • 

  • not a wall-clock proof that the list preceded the results: the seal precedes the texts in chain order, and the chain existed before one bitcoin block per day (pending tonight); the header's own sentence says timestamps are the gate's statement, not evidence of order, and a chain restarted before its first stamp leaves no trace outside (one smoke chain was deleted tonight before the started line). a batch list stamped before planting would be the stronger seal; not built.
    • 

  • not a verdict on the reader's promise where the form is an open question: those forms sit behind flags the lab ships OFF by a recorded decision, or are disclosed as unread, or sit under no rule; the totals are observed and printed, and a repair that moves them is an amendment with a date.
    • 

  • not a stranger's chain: the same lab wrote both chains on the same box, this one with the gate's code as copied at 2026-11-08T22:46:08Z. what this shows is that the format re-derives under the same verifier and the same vectors for a second writer; a chain written elsewhere by someone else would show more.
    • 

  • not anchored in a chat: the anchor text is a stamped file on the exhibit branch that names no ledger; the day's anchor line is owed after the day closes.
    • 

  • not the header's own words: the header's certifies sentence is the gate code's fixed one, inherited by the copy ("never message text or chat ids" holds: the lines carry hashes and counts); the sentence that says what THIS chain is stands at the top of this page, not in the header.
    • 

  • not one gate digest: gate_sha256 on the lines folds the plugin folder in; the lines before RED_AMENDMENT rule 6 hashed the live plugin folder as it stood, later lines hash the copy's own. a change of that digest between two red lines is the amendment, dated, not a change of the gate code (STATS.json gate_sha256_on_lines).
