The value that ends up stored in `x` is **3.0**.

Here's why:

1.  **Integer Division:** The expression `7 / 2` involves two integer literals (`7` and `2`). In C (and many other languages), when both operands of the division operator (`/`) are integers, C performs **integer division**.
    *   Integer division calculates how many times the divisor goes into the dividend, discarding any remainder.
    *   `7 / 2` evaluates to `3` (since 2 goes into 7 three times, with a remainder of 1, which is discarded).

2.  **Type Conversion:** The result of `7 / 2` is the integer `3`. This integer `3` is then assigned to the `double` variable `x`.
    *   When an integer is assigned to a `double` variable, C automatically converts the integer to its floating-point equivalent.
    *   So, the integer `3` is converted to `3.0`.

Therefore, `x` will store `3.0`.
